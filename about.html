Course Description

Discover how code can be used to create, communicate, and shape the world around us. Learn foundational programming concepts and computational thinking strategies while designing interactive projects and developing a portfolio of your work. Use algorithms, formal logic, quantitative information, and computational models to solve problems, represent relationships, and evaluate results. Critically examine how data-driven and algorithmic systems affect individuals, communities, and global, social, and political life, including issues of bias, privacy, access, and ethical responsibility. Through creative experimentation, problem-solving, and reflection, use code to explore real-world questions, challenges, and ideas.

Learning Objectives

  • Define and use foundational computational and programming terminology accurately.
  • Apply decomposition, pattern recognition, abstraction, algorithms, mathematical reasoning, and formal logic to solve problems in computing and other contexts.
  • Represent and critically interpret data, quantitative relationships, and computational processes using words, code, symbolic expressions, diagrams, formulas, tables, and visualizations, recognizing the assumptions and limitations of different representations.
  • Predict and estimate values, ranges, outputs, or program behaviors, and compare expected and observed results to evaluate their plausibility.
  • Apply programming concepts, logic, and syntax to design, test, debug, and iteratively improve accessible and inclusive interactive applications.
  • Analyze how computational systems reflect, reproduce, or challenge social inequities and structures of power, including issues involving algorithmic bias, privacy, accessibility, representation, and unequal access.
  • Create, document, present, and reflect on a portfolio of computational projects that address real-world questions, challenges, or ideas.
